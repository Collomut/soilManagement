SECTION 01 PROJECT IDENTITY

1.1 What is the project called and what does the name mean

Design Specification The formal project title is Zerthiva: Soil Intelligence System (commonly abbreviated as Zerthiva or SIS). The name "Zerthiva" derives from the convergence of zero-degradation agriculture (*Zer-*), the terrestrial root zone (*-th-* / *terra*), and biological vitality (*-viva*). It designates an in-situ agricultural diagnostic and decision-support instrument engineered to safeguard arable land from invisible root-zone degradation, specifically the gradual, unmonitored accumulation of dissolved mineral salts (secondary salinization) that smallholder farmers cannot visually detect until crop yield is irreversibly compromised.

1.2 One-sentence description

Design Specification Zerthiva is an affordable, field-deployed root-zone monitoring node and edge-decision system that measures apparent bulk soil electrical conductivity ($EC_a$), volumetric moisture, and soil temperature every 15 minutes, alerting smallholder farmers to salinity thresholds and soil water depletion via intuitive local visual beacons and plain-language SMS guidance.

1.3 Core idea

Established Evidence Three fundamental root-zone variables—soil salinity, plant-available moisture, and applied fertilizer ions—are physically and chemically coupled in irrigated agriculture (FAO-29; FAO-56). In arid and semi-arid regions, irrigating with mineral-bearing water without measuring root-zone electrical conductivity inevitably concentrates salts in the transpiration zone as water evaporates. Conversely, applying synthetic chemical fertilizers (which are ionic mineral salts) to an already saline root zone induces osmotic drought, reversing the osmotic pressure gradient and preventing plant roots from extracting water even in water-saturated soil.

Design Specification Zerthiva provides smallholder farmers with an affordable in-situ screening tool that continuously monitors these interdependent root-zone dynamics, removing guesswork and preventing farmers from compounding salinity damage through misdiagnosed water and fertilizer applications.

1.4 Agricultural sector targeted

Zerthiva primarily addresses smallholder irrigated crop production, specifically targeting:

  • Irrigation Water Management: Transitioning from calendar-based over-watering to data-informed moisture replenishment based on soil water depletion ($d_{net}$ in mm).
  • Input & Fertilizer Stewardship: Preventing toxic fertilizer top-dressing during periods of elevated soil ionic concentration via an automated salinity screening lockout.
  • Land Preservation: Early detection of secondary salinization in vulnerable irrigated river basins and groundwater-irrigated perimeters.

1.5 Geographic focus and target agro-ecological zone

Established Evidence The primary geographical target for prototype validation is the arid and semi-arid lands (ASALs) of Eastern Kenya (Machakos County, Central and Yatta sub-counties), situated within the Upper Athi River catchment. In this agro-ecological zone, smallholder farmers cultivate high-value vegetables (tomatoes, capsicums) and staple maize under furrow, basin, and drip irrigation using shallow alluvial groundwater, seasonal sand dams, and river diversions.

Established Evidence Published soil surveys and hydrological datasets for this corridor (Gicheru & Ita, 1987; KALRO Katumani station records; Machakos CIDP 2023–2027) document that shallow irrigation aquifers and river diversions in the Athi basin exhibit seasonal electrical conductivity swings between 0.6 and 2.4 dS/m during dry-season low-flow periods, placing irrigation water in the "slight to moderate" and "severe" salinity hazard classifications according to Ayers & Westcot (1985, FAO-29).

1.6 Primary and secondary users

Primary Users: Smallholder irrigators cultivating 0.5 to 2.5 hectares who make daily operational decisions regarding pump scheduling and fertilizer top-dressing, often with limited formal technical or literacy training.

Secondary Users: Cooperative agronomists, public agricultural extension officers (ward agricultural officers), and catchment management associations who require aggregated spatial telemetry to track regional groundwater salinity trajectories.

In Phase 2 (Commercialization), hardware procurement is structured through agricultural producer cooperatives, where the node cost ($35–$45) is financed over a full growing season or bundled into cooperative input advances. SECTION 02 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

2.1 The invisible root-zone crisis

Established Evidence Across the arid and semi-arid irrigation corridors of Sub-Saharan Africa, smallholder farmers face a silent, accelerating threat: secondary soil salinization. Irrigating with saline groundwater or river diversions without monitoring soil electrical conductivity draws dissolved mineral salts into the transpiration zone. As pure water evaporates, these salts accumulate, driving total soil water potential ($\Psi_{total} = \Psi_m + \Psi_s$) strongly negative and inducing osmotic drought. Plants wilt despite saturated soil moisture.

2.2 The destructive feedback loop

Unvalidated Behavioral Hypothesis In the absence of diagnostic soil sensing, farmers routinely misinterpret osmotic wilting as simple water deficiency. Field observations and agricultural surveys (Qadir et al., 2014) indicate that farmers frequently respond by increasing irrigation pumping frequency (delivering more dissolved mineral salts) and applying synthetic chemical fertilizers such as CAN or urea. Because synthetic fertilizers are mineral salts, this misdiagnosed intervention sharply spikes root-zone electrical conductivity, accelerating osmotic lockout and resulting in 15% to 50% yield collapse or complete plot abandonment. This behavioral feedback loop represents a central hypothesis to be empirically evaluated during baseline pilot recruitment.

2.3 The Zerthiva intervention

Design Specification Zerthiva intervenes with an autonomous, field-deployed $30–$35 hardware node and an edge-computing decision framework. Embedded at 15 cm root-zone depth, the node continuously measures apparent bulk soil conductivity ($EC_a$), volumetric moisture content (VWC), and soil temperature ($T_{soil}$). The firmware normalizes conductivity to 25°C ($EC_{25}$) and applies a soil-specific moisture-dependent conversion model (Rhoades et al., 1989) to estimate saturated paste extract salinity ($EC_e$) when volumetric moisture $\theta \ge 0.15\text{ m}^3/\text{m}^3$. If soil moisture is dry ($\theta < 0.15$), the system inhibits threshold breach alerts and displays a conservative dry-soil screening status.

2.4 Decoupled agronomic logic

Established Evidence The decision engine rigorously decouples water balance from salinity dynamics:

  • Irrigation Scheduling (FAO-56): Calculates net irrigation requirement ($d_{net} = \frac{MAD \times TAW}{100}$ in mm) based on measured depletion of readily available water ($RAW$), eliminating arbitrary universal runtime instructions.
  • Salinity Screening & Lockout (Maas-Hoffman 1977 / FAO-29): Evaluates estimated $EC_e$ against crop-specific thresholds ($a = 1.7\text{ dS/m}$ for Maize; $a = 2.5\text{ dS/m}$ for Tomato). When estimated $EC_e \ge a$, the node triggers an automated Salinity Lockout Alert, advising the farmer to withhold synthetic top-dressing and inspect water quality.
  • Salt Leaching Safety: Leaching alerts are constrained by soil drainage capacity and waterlogging lockouts, requiring that irrigation water salinity ($EC_w$) is low enough to effectively dilute root-zone salts ($EC_w < EC_e / 2$).

SECTION 03 INTRODUCTION

3.1 Global scale of the salinization challenge

Established Evidence Land degradation via secondary salinization affects an estimated 1.5 million hectares of arable land annually worldwide, with cumulative global economic losses in agricultural productivity exceeding $27 billion per year (Qadir et al., 2014; FAO, 2021). The problem is most acute across arid and semi-arid irrigated perimeters, where high atmospheric evaporative demand ($ET_0 > 1,500\text{ mm/year}$) combines with mineralized irrigation water to concentrate salts in the upper 30 cm of the soil profile.

3.2 Sub-Saharan African and Kenyan context

Established Evidence In Kenya, arid and semi-arid lands (ASALs) comprise over 80% of the total landmass. Smallholder agriculture accounts for approximately 75% of total agricultural output and over 70% of rural employment (Kenya National Bureau of Statistics, Economic Survey 2024). In counties such as Machakos, Makueni, and Kitui, the expansion of small-scale irrigation along river perimeters (e.g., Athi River) is critical for local food security and household incomes. However, unmonitored abstraction from alluvial aquifers exhibiting elevated seasonal electrical conductivity (0.6 to 2.4 dS/m) threatens long-term soil productivity.

3.3 The physical monitoring divide

Established Evidence While satellite remote sensing (e.g., Sentinel-2 NDVI, Landsat thermal imagery) provides valuable macro-scale vegetative indices, satellite data detects crop stress only after cellular chlorophyll degradation and canopy wilting have already occurred. Satellite sensors cannot measure in-situ root-zone pore water salinity or distinguish osmotic drought from physical water deficiency. Ground-based continuous root-zone monitoring is physically essential for pre-symptomatic detection.

SECTION 04 BACKGROUND OF THE PROBLEM

4.1 The physics of root-zone salt accumulation

Established Evidence When irrigation water containing dissolved mineral ions ($Ca^{2+}, Mg^{2+}, Na^+, Cl^-, SO_4^{2-}, HCO_3^-$) is applied to soil, crop roots extract almost pure water ($H_2O$) through transpiration, while water also evaporates directly from the soil surface. Virtually all dissolved mineral ions remain in the root zone.

Design Specification Illustrative Mass-Balance Approximation: If an irrigator applies 500 mm ($5,000\text{ m}^3/\text{ha}$) of water across a season from a source with an electrical conductivity of $1.5\text{ dS/m}$ ($TDS \approx 960\text{ mg/L}$ based on standard conversion $TDS \approx 640 \times EC$), each hectare receives approximately:

Mass of Salt Deposited = 5,000,000 L/ha × 0.96 g/L = 4,800,000 g = 4,800 kg/ha (4.8 metric tons/ha)

Unless sufficient leaching water passes through the root zone and exits via adequate drainage, this mass of salt accumulates in the top 30 cm of soil, rapidly elevating root-zone salinity.

4.2 The destructive feedback cycle

Unvalidated Behavioral Hypothesis Below is the 6-stage progressive cycle of unmonitored root-zone salinization commonly observed in irrigated smallholder plots:

Stage 1
Intuition-Based Irrigation

Farmer irrigates on a rigid calendar schedule or when topsoil feels dry, applying unmeasured volumes of water containing trace dissolved mineral salts.

Stage 2
Subsurface Salt Accumulation

Pure water evaporates under intense tropical sunlight, leaving mineral ions behind. Evaporative capillary draw concentrates salts directly in the upper 15–30 cm transpiration zone.

Stage 3
Osmotic Root Blockade

High Electrical Conductivity ($EC_e$) drives osmotic water potential strongly negative ($\Psi_s \ll 0$). Crop root hairs cannot extract water, causing physiological drought in moist soil.

Stage 4
Farmer Misdiagnosis

The farmer observes stunted stalks, leaf chlorosis, and wilting. Unable to see root-zone electrical conductivity, they assume crops are thirsty or nitrogen-deficient.

Stage 5
Compound Over-Application

The farmer pumps more irrigation water and dumps synthetic chemical fertilizers (CAN/urea). Because fertilizers are mineral salts, root-zone salinity spikes to lethal levels.

Stage 6
Crop Collapse & Land Degradation

Roots suffer irreversible osmotic plasmolysis and toxic ion injury. Up to 50% of harvest is lost, input capital is wasted, and topsoil develops alkali salt crusts.

🛡️ How Zerthiva Permanently Breaks This Vicious Cycle

Zerthiva intercepts this crisis at Stage 3 before visible cellular wilting occurs. Embedded at 15 cm depth, the node continuously monitors apparent bulk conductivity ($EC_a$) and soil water depletion ($RAW$). When estimated $EC_e$ approaches or breaches the crop tolerance threshold ($a = 1.7\text{ dS/m}$ for Maize, $2.5\text{ dS/m}$ for Tomato), the system immediately triggers the sentry LED to Amber/Red and sends an SMS alert: "Hold Fertilizer — High Soil Salinity Detected. Do not top-dress. Inspect water source." Simultaneously, it calculates net irrigation replenishment ($d_{net}$ in mm) to satisfy crop water requirements without over-irrigation, preserving roots and saving the harvest.

4.3 Sodicity hazards and soil structure

Established Evidence In addition to osmotic stress, elevated sodium concentrations relative to calcium and magnesium can cause sodicity hazards. The Sodium Adsorption Ratio ($SAR = \frac{[Na^+]}{\sqrt{([Ca^{2+}] + [Mg^{2+}])/2}}$) indicates the risk of clay dispersion, soil crusting, and reduced hydraulic conductivity. However, sodicity vulnerability varies significantly with clay mineralogy (e.g., highly vulnerable smectites vs. more stable kaolinites typical of weathered tropical soils) and total electrolyte concentration (Ayers & Westcot, 1985). Zerthiva notes this relationship as context, while scoping its initial prototype specifically to total electrical conductivity monitoring.

SECTION 05 PROBLEM STATEMENT

5.1 The specific problem Zerthiva addresses

Design Specification Smallholder farmers in arid and semi-arid irrigation schemes lack affordable, continuous diagnostic tools to detect root-zone salinity accumulation ($EC_a$) and soil water depletion ($RAW$) in real time. Consequently, farmers cannot distinguish between osmotic drought (caused by elevated salt concentration) and matric drought (caused by physical water depletion).

5.2 System boundary & measurement scope

Design Specification Zerthiva measures in-situ apparent bulk electrical conductivity ($EC_a$) at a single representative root-zone depth (15 cm). It does not directly perform certified laboratory chemical extraction ($EC_e$), nor does it measure individual nitrogen, phosphorus, or potassium ion concentrations. Instead, it utilizes an empirical soil-specific model (Rhoades et al., 1989) to estimate $EC_e$ as a screening indicator to alert farmers when root-zone salinity approaches published crop tolerance thresholds ($a = 1.7\text{ dS/m}$ for Maize; $a = 2.5\text{ dS/m}$ for Tomato, Maas & Hoffman, 1977).

5.3 Consequences of unmonitored management

Established Evidence When root-zone salinity exceeds crop tolerance thresholds, crop yield declines linearly according to the Maas-Hoffman relationship ($12.0\%$ per dS/m for Maize, $9.9\%$ per dS/m for Tomato). In field conditions where salinity reaches 3.5 to 5.0 dS/m, yield losses range from 20% to over 50% (Ayers & Westcot, 1985). Misapplying synthetic fertilizer during such periods exacerbates root osmotic stress, causing severe scorching and potential total crop failure.

SECTION 06 ROOT-CAUSE ANALYSIS

6.1 Five Whys vertical root-cause chain

Why #1
Why do smallholder farmers lose 15%–40% of their harvest to salinity?

Because salinity accumulates invisibly in the root zone. By the time visual signs appear (leaf scorch, chlorosis, stunting), irreversible root cellular damage and yield loss have already occurred.

Why #2
Why are smallholders unaware that salts are building up?

Because they possess zero in-situ diagnostic tools. Soil pore-water solution chemistry is completely invisible to the human eye during daily farm operations.

Why #3
Why don't farmers use existing commercial soil testing tools?

Commercial laboratory soil tests cost $30–$80 per sample and require 2–4 weeks for mail returns. Commercial IoT telemetry stations cost $1,200–$2,800, completely out of reach for a smallholder earning $3–$6/day.

Why #4
Why haven't low-cost agricultural sensors been built until now?

Commodity components only reached viable sub-$10 price thresholds recently. Microcontrollers like the ESP32 ($3–$5), low-noise capacitive sensors, and corrosion-resistant SS316 probes now make a sub-$35 node technically feasible.

Why #5
Why hasn't low-cost hardware solved this problem on its own?

Because raw hardware is not a solution. Smallholders require an autonomous edge decision engine that translates raw electrical sensor millivolts into plain-language actions (irrigation depth $d_{net}$ in mm, salinity lockout alerts) delivered through zero-literacy beacons without requiring expensive smartphones or cloud connectivity.

SECTION 09 EXISTING SOLUTIONS AND MEASUREMENT PHYSICS

9.1 Rigorous definitions of electrical conductivity metrics

Established Evidence To avoid scientific ambiguity, the documentation formally distinguishes five distinct electrical conductivity metrics:

Metric Designation Measurement Method / Physical Definition Agronomic Role
$EC_a$ Apparent Bulk Soil EC In-situ bulk conductivity measured directly by probe electrodes embedded in moist soil. Includes solid matrix, clay surface conductance, and pore solution. Raw physical sensor output.
$EC_{25}$ Temperature-Normalized Bulk EC Bulk apparent conductivity normalized to a standard 25.0°C reference using linear compensation: $EC_{25} = EC_a / [1 + 0.0191 \times (T - 25.0)]$. Standardized bulk measurement.
$EC_p$ Soil Pore-Water EC Electrical conductivity of the liquid solution contained within soil pores, extracted or estimated via Rhoades / Hilhorst models. True salinity felt by root hairs.
$EC_e$ Saturated Paste Extract EC Electrical conductivity of water extracted from a laboratory saturated soil paste (standard reference method). Reference metric for all published crop salinity thresholds.
$EC_{1:5}$ 1:5 Soil-to-Water Extract EC Conductivity of a suspension of 1 part dry soil to 5 parts distilled water. Relates to $EC_e$ via texture-dependent multiplier ($6.4–8.0$, Sonmez et al., 2008). Rapid laboratory screening metric.

9.2 The mathematical measurement and conversion chain

Design Specification Zerthiva executes a rigorous 5-step conversion chain from raw voltage to agronomic screening advisory:

Step 1: Voltage to Bulk Resistance: R_s = R_div × [ (V_ref / V_ADC) - 1 ] Step 2: Resistance to Bulk Apparent Conductivity (EC_a): EC_a = K_cell / R_s Step 3: Temperature Normalization (25°C): EC_25 = EC_a / [ 1.0 + 0.0191 × (T_soil - 25.0) ] Step 4: Soil-Specific Conversion to Pore Solution Salinity (Rhoades et al., 1989): EC_p = (EC_25 - EC_s) / [ θ × (a × θ + b) ] (Valid only when θ ≥ 0.15 m³/m³; inhibited when θ < 0.15 m³/m³) Step 5: Estimated Saturated Paste Salinity (EC_e): EC_e_est ≈ EC_p × (θ_FC / θ_SP) ≈ 0.5 × EC_p (for typical loam soils)
Operational Validity Constraint
When volumetric water content is dry ($\theta < 0.15\text{ m}^3/\text{m}^3$), the liquid water film bridging the probe electrodes becomes discontinuous, contact resistance spikes nonlinearly, and bulk-to-solution models break down. In this condition, the firmware inhibits crop threshold comparisons and flags the output as STATUS_DRY_SOIL_SCREENING_ONLY.

SECTION 11 PROPOSED SOLUTION AND CORE FUNCTIONS

11.1 Decoupled multi-state internal logic

Design Specification To ensure agricultural safety and unambiguous guidance, the Zerthiva firmware maintains separate internal state variables rather than collapsing disparate physical conditions into a single arbitrary number:

  • State 1: Salinity Status ($S_{sal}$): Evaluates estimated saturated extract salinity ($EC_{e\_est}$) relative to crop threshold $a$. (Values: SAFE, CAUTION, CRITICAL_LOCKOUT, UNRELIABLE_DRY).
  • State 2: Soil Water Depletion ($S_{wat}$): Evaluates volumetric moisture ($\theta$) relative to Field Capacity ($\theta_{FC}$) and Management Allowed Depletion ($MAD$). (Values: SATURATED, OPTIMAL, DEPLETED_IRRIGATE, WILTING_STRESS).
  • State 3: Sensor Diagnostic Quality ($S_{diag}$): Evaluates electrode electrical continuity, temperature validity (-5°C to 65°C), and measurement noise. (Values: VALID, DEGRADED, DISCONNECTED).
  • State 4: Power & Battery Health ($S_{pwr}$): Monitors LiPo voltage ($V_{bat}$). (Values: NORMAL_3.8V+, LOW_3.5V, CRITICAL_SLEEP_3.4V).

15.2 Three formalized operational modes

Design Specification To guarantee resilience across varying rural infrastructure environments, Zerthiva implements three distinct operational modes:

18.2 The Rhoades bulk-to-solution conversion model

Established Evidence In-situ probes measure bulk apparent conductivity ($EC_a$). To compare with published $EC_e$ thresholds, Zerthiva implements the physical conversion model formulated by Rhoades et al. (1989):

EC_a = [ θ × (a × θ + b) ] × EC_p + EC_s where: - EC_a = measured in-situ bulk apparent conductivity normalized to 25°C - θ = volumetric water content (m³/m³) - a, b = empirical transmission parameters specific to soil texture - EC_p = pore-water solution electrical conductivity - EC_s = solid clay mineral surface conductance At field capacity (θ ≈ θ_FC), the estimated saturated paste extract salinity is: EC_e_est ≈ EC_p × (θ_FC / θ_SP) ≈ 0.5 × EC_p (for typical loam soils)
Safety Scope & Operational Fallback
The Rhoades model is valid only when soil moisture is sufficient to maintain continuous pore water films ($\theta \ge 0.15\text{ m}^3/\text{m}^3$). If $\theta < 0.15$, the conversion is suspended, threshold breach alerts are inhibited, and the system prompts the farmer for clean irrigation water.

29.2 Exploratory feasibility framing & statistical plan

Design Specification Audit Finding Addressed: With $N = 5$ matched pairs, the minimum possible two-sided p-value for an exact Wilcoxon signed-rank test is $2 \times (1/2)^5 = 0.0625$, which cannot achieve a conventional $p < 0.05$ two-sided threshold. Therefore, the 10-farm pilot is formally defined as an exploratory feasibility trial. The primary statistical analysis reports:

  1. Paired Mean Differences ($\bar{D}$): Mean difference between treatment and control pairs with 90% confidence intervals for water consumption ($m^3/\text{ha}$) and harvest yield ($t/\text{ha}$).
  2. Standardized Effect Sizes: Cohen's $d_z$ and Hedge's $g$ to quantify the practical magnitude of the intervention.
  3. Directional One-Sided Hypothesis Exploration: One-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank testing ($p_{\min} = 1/32 = 0.03125 < 0.05$) to evaluate directional hypotheses (Treatment Water $\le$ Control Water; Treatment Yield $\ge$ Control Yield).
  4. Power Calculation for Confirmatory Phase 2: Establishing pilot variance to size a confirmatory trial ($N \ge 16$ pairs required for 80% power at $\alpha = 0.05$, two-sided, $d = 0.75$).

57.1 The calibrated investment thesis

  1. Q1: How do you measure soil EC with an $8.00 probe, and how does that relate to crop thresholds?
    Answer: The probe measures apparent bulk electrical conductivity ($EC_a$) using 1kHz AC excitation. Bulk $EC_a$ is not identical to saturated paste extract ($EC_e$) used in crop thresholds. Firmware normalizes $EC_a$ to 25°C ($EC_{25}$) and applies the Rhoades (1989) model to estimate $EC_e$ when soil moisture $\theta \ge 0.15\text{ m}^3/\text{m}^3$. In dry soils ($\theta < 0.15$), threshold alerts are inhibited and the system flags status as dry-soil screening only.
  2. Q2: Why did you remove the claimed "±5% accuracy" figure?
    Answer: Because a universal ±5% accuracy claim across all soil types is physically indefensible without multi-point soil-specific calibration. We replaced it with an empirical bench target in standard KCl ($R^2 \ge 0.99, \text{repeatability } \pm 0.15\text{ dS/m}$) and a calibrated soil model RMSE target $\le 0.35\text{ dS/m}$.
  3. Q3: How have you decoupled FAO-56 from salinity and fertilizer logic?
    Answer: FAO-56 is strictly applied to soil water balance and net irrigation depth ($d_{net} = \frac{MAD \times TAW}{100}$ in mm). Crop salinity thresholds are governed independently by Maas-Hoffman (1977), and leaching fractions by Ayers & Westcot (1985, FAO-29).
  4. Q4: Why have you eliminated arbitrary irrigation durations like "water for 45 minutes"?
    Answer: Because runtime depends entirely on irrigation system emitter discharge rate ($q$), operating pressure, and plot area ($A$), which a single soil probe cannot know. The node outputs net irrigation depth ($d_{net}$) in millimeters.
  5. Q5: Can bulk EC determine chemical fertilizer dosages (NPK)?
    Answer: No. Bulk EC measures total dissolved ionic conductivity, not individual nitrogen, phosphorus, or potassium concentrations. Zerthiva eliminates automatic dosage formulas, utilizing EC strictly as an automated Salinity Lockout Alert to withhold synthetic top-dressing during salt accumulation events.
  6. Q6: What prevents automatic salt-leaching recommendations from waterlogging crops?
    Answer: The decision engine enforces drainage checks and a waterlogging lockout, requiring that irrigation water salinity ($EC_w$) is low enough to dilute root-zone salts ($EC_w \le 0.5 \times EC_e$) and prohibiting leaching in poorly drained soils or high water tables.
  7. Q7: Does withholding fertilizer prevent blossom-end rot (BER) in tomato?
    Answer: BER is a physiological disorder linked to localized calcium deficiency in fruit tissue, often exacerbated by high root-zone salinity (which impedes $Ca^{2+}$ uptake) and irregular watering. Withholding nitrogen top-dressing during salinity spikes reduces osmotic competition and excessive vegetative growth, but is framed as a risk-mitigation hypothesis, not a guaranteed cure.
  8. Q8: What is the exact development stage of Zerthiva?
    Answer: Technology Readiness Level 4 (TRL 4) — Functional Bench Prototype. Circuit prototypes have been validated in aqueous standards and laboratory soil test columns. Field efficacy will be validated during the Machakos pilot.
  9. Q9: How do you achieve secure cellular telemetry over a SIM800L 2G modem?
    Answer: Telemetry JSON payloads are signed using application-layer HMAC-SHA256 with monotonic sequence counters to prevent spoofing and replay. GPRS HTTP uses TLS 1.2 on ESP32 mbedTLS. True native TLS 1.3 is mapped to the next-gen LTE-M (SIM7080G) hardware roadmap.
  10. Q10: How do you explain the power budget and the 340-hour battery test?

Base logging draws $4.28\text{ mAh/day}$ (448 days reserve on 1,920 mAh net). Standard 4 SMS syncs/day draws $13.28\text{ mAh/day}$ (144.5 days reserve). The 340.8-hour (14.2-day) dark chamber test was an extreme stress test under continuous hourly transmission with poor signal retries. Both vastly exceed the $\ge 72\text{-hour}$ zero-solar design requirement (NFR01).

Agronomic Advisory Scope & Screening Disclaimer Zerthiva is an in-situ agricultural screening and operational decision-support tool. Estimated saturated paste extract salinity ($EC_e$) and net irrigation depths ($d_{net}$) are mathematical approximations derived from apparent bulk soil measurements ($EC_a$, VWC) and published empirical models (Rhoades et al., 1989; FAO-56). Zerthiva does not directly measure individual chemical nutrient ions (nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium) and is not a certified legal substitute for formal laboratory soil and water analysis. Farmers should consult qualified agricultural extension officers and certified laboratories prior to major land reclamation interventions.
